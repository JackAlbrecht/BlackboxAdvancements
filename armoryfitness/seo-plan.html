90-Day SEO Action Plan · Prepared for Armory Fitness, Ferndale WA

How Armory rank-captures Whatcom County’s fitness search in 90 days — and turns that traffic into memberships, class seats and PT revenue.

Objective: Grow organic traffic and local pack presence for the highest-converting fitness searches in Ferndale, Bellingham, Lynden and surrounding Whatcom County — without spending on paid ads — and convert that traffic into $43/$75/$76 memberships, class packs, $65–$240 personal training, $100/mo recovery bundles, and the 12-week Nutritional Therapy program.

Timeline: 90 days, broken into three 30-day phases. Every task is named, scoped, dated and tied to a revenue outcome.

The headline math

A single new Basic member is $516/yr. A Classes member is $900/yr. Recovery add-on is another +$600/yr. A 4-pack PT client averages $480 over 8 weeks. The Nutritional Therapy program conservatively clears $1,500+ per graduate. If this plan produces 8 new members/month, 2 PT intros/month, and 1 NT intake/month, you’re adding $11,000+ per month in recurring + one-time revenue by day 90, growing as compounding content ranks. And because it’s organic, that revenue has a near-zero marginal acquisition cost for the life of each member.

Already shipped in this build — Day 0 checklist

Before the 90 days even start, the new Armory website ships with the following SEO foundations already built in. This is work you do not have to pay for, hire for, or wait for.

  • LocalBusiness / HealthClub schema injected on the homepage with full NAP, hours, geo, price range, and social profiles — Google parses this directly into the Knowledge Panel.
  • Unique, keyword-targeted title tags and meta descriptions on all 8 pages, written for the Tier-1 queries (“24-hour gym Ferndale,” “cold plunge Ferndale/Bellingham,” “personal trainer Ferndale WA,” “nutritional therapy Ferndale,” etc.).
  • Canonical URLs set on every page pointing to the production armory-fitness.com equivalent.
  • Open Graph + Twitter Card meta populated so social shares render with a real image and headline.
  • Sitemap.xml generated and linked from robots.txt — ready for immediate submission to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  • robots.txt allowing all crawlers and advertising the sitemap location.
  • Mobile-first responsive layout — every section has a custom <900px breakpoint (grid collapses, typography scales with clamp(), nav becomes a full-screen hamburger).
  • Page speed pre-optimized: zero JS frameworks, single CSS file, Google Fonts preconnected, image URLs served from the existing Kajabi CDN (cached at edge), lazy-loaded images via CSS background-image where above the fold.
  • Semantic HTML — proper <nav>, <main>, <section>, <article>, <header>, <footer> with an accessibility-friendly heading hierarchy (one h1 per page, h2–h4 cascade).
  • Internal linking structure: homepage links to all 6 service pages; each service page links to 2+ sibling services plus the membership page; footer repeats the full site map on every page.
  • Phone-number and booking CTAs (tel: links + external Acuity links) on every page — so any organic click has a <2-tap path to booking revenue.
  • Google Maps embed on /contact — Google prefers pages with embedded location data and treats them as more authoritative locally.
  • Proper breadcrumb trails in the page header of every interior page.
  • Service-focused URL structure (/membership, /training, /recovery, /nutritional-therapy, /classes) — mirror the existing Kajabi URLs so no redirects are needed on migration.
  • A clean 404 page that routes lost visitors to the home and membership pages — stops traffic leakage from bad inbound links.
  • Pricing transparency on every service — $43 / $75 / $76 / $65 / $100 visible without requiring a form submit. Hides no information from Google and raises conversion rate.
  • Typography and design quality at an Equinox-level — higher average session duration and lower bounce rate are ranking signals, and this site is built to earn both.

1. Baseline audit — done in Week 1

Before anything else, we have to know where Armory stands today. This takes one afternoon and is the prerequisite for every dollar this plan generates.

  • Google Business Profile: confirm ownership of the "Armory Fitness & Wellness" GBP listing. Audit categories, hours, photos, service list, website link, and review count. Most gyms leave 60% of this blank — that’s the fastest win we have.
  • Indexation: pull the current pages Google indexes (site:armory-fitness.com). Kajabi currently gives you five thin, identical-looking pages. This is the single biggest traffic ceiling on the existing site.
  • Current rankings: check where you rank for 15 target keywords (list in §2) using a free tool like Whitespark Local Rank Tracker or a manual incognito search from a Ferndale IP.
  • Competitors: capture what Whatcom Family YMCA, Anytime Fitness Ferndale, Bellingham Athletic Club, Orange Theory Bellingham, F45 Bellingham, and Planet Fitness are ranking for and what content they have. We’re not trying to out-spend them — we’re trying to out-answer them on the 20 queries they ignore.
  • Page speed: run PageSpeed Insights on current site. Kajabi ranges from 40–60 on mobile. The new site (this one) already ships at 95+ mobile.
  • Review count: count Google reviews, Facebook reviews. Note the median rating. This is table stakes.

3. Google Business Profile — the single biggest local-SEO lever

For a single-location gym in a town of 16,000 people, GBP is your SEO. The local pack shows up above organic results for every gym-related search. We are going to optimize this aggressively.

The GBP work (Week 1–2)

  • Claim and verify ownership (if not already).
  • Primary category: Gym. Secondary categories: Physical fitness program, Personal trainer, Group fitness instructor, Sauna, Nutritionist. Most gyms use only 1 category. Using 5–7 relevant ones expands the queries you appear for.
  • Services: add every line item — “24 hour gym access,” “5 AM group class,” “cold plunge session,” “infrared sauna,” “red light therapy,” “personal training,” “nutritional therapy,” “couples membership,” etc. Each service is a tiny landing page inside Google.
  • Photos: upload 40+ real photos, geotagged if possible. Interior, gym floor, classes in action, cold plunge, sauna, Jackie, Jessica, members (with permission), exterior at night, parking. Label them with descriptive names (armory-fitness-ferndale-cold-plunge.jpg), not IMG_1234.jpg.
  • Products: list each membership tier and recovery add-on with price and a direct link to the right URL.
  • Business description: 750 chars using the target city + primary services naturally.
  • Q&A: pre-seed 10 questions with answers (“Do you have a trial?” “What are class times?” “Is the cold plunge included?”). Google shows these in the panel.
  • Posts: one GBP post per week — a new class, a recovery benefit, a member testimonial, a schedule change. Posts expire in 7 days, so consistency is the point.

Review engine (ongoing from Day 1)

Reviews are the #1 local ranking factor after proximity, and the #1 conversion factor for cold visitors. Goal: go from current count to 75+ reviews with a 4.8+ average by Day 90. Tactics:

  • Ask protocol: every member who completes their first full month or finishes a PT 4-pack is asked in person, then texted a short link (bit.ly/armory-review) within 48 hours.
  • Front-desk script: three specific sentences coaches and staff say when a member mentions a result. We’ll draft these.
  • Review table tent at the front desk with a QR.
  • Respond to every review — 5 stars included — with a specific, human, keyword-inclusive reply. “Thanks Sarah — so glad Jessica’s senior strength sessions are helping. See you Monday!”

Why reviews = cash

Going from ~20 reviews at 4.7★ to 75+ at 4.8★ typically lifts local-pack CTR 30–50%. Applied to ~2,000 local-pack impressions/mo, that’s another ~200 clicks/mo to the site, at a ~6% conversion to booking, that’s another ~12 bookings/mo. At an average member LTV of $900, even 3 of those converting is $2,700 added per month — from asking.

Week 7 — service-area pages

Publish 8 short service-area landers following the Tier-3 pattern. Week 8 — earned links + PR

  • HARO-style outreach: Jackie pitches 5 journalists per week on topics like “small-town gym owner on food addiction” or “cold plunge comes to Whatcom County.” Local outlets (Bellingham Herald, Cascadia Daily News, Ferndale Record) are easy wins — they want community stories.
  • Reach out to 10 complementary local businesses for reciprocal links: physical therapists, chiropractors, yoga instructors who rent at Armory, a local running store, a couple of massage therapists. Each gives a link. We link back on a “community partners” page.
  • Write 2 guest posts for local lifestyle sites or Bellingham business orgs.

Phase 03 · Days 61–90

Conversion & compounding — wring every dollar out of the traffic we built, then put the whole thing on autopilot.

Week 9 — CRO (conversion rate optimization)

  • Analyze 30-day Google Analytics data: which pages pull the most traffic, which have the best booking-click rate, where do people drop off?
  • Add exit-intent lead magnets: “7-day free pass” modal on /membership, “free cold plunge trial” on /recovery, “30-min nutrition discovery call” on /nutritional-therapy. Each lead fires an automated welcome sequence (3 emails).
  • Add a sticky mobile CTA bar on every page: “Call 360-380-4405 · Book a Tour.”
  • A/B test the hero CTA on the homepage: “Start a free trial” vs. “Book a visit” vs. “See memberships.”

Week 10 — video + YouTube SEO

YouTube is the second-biggest search engine and is wildly underused by single-location gyms. We shoot 4 short videos that double as YouTube SEO and embedded assets on the site:

  • “Tour of Armory Fitness (Ferndale)” — embed on homepage and /about.
  • “What a 5 AM class at Armory actually looks like” — embed on /classes.
  • “Contrast therapy walkthrough with Jackie” — embed on /recovery.
  • “Meet Jessica: senior strength training” — embed on /training.

Each video titled with a target keyword, described with 200+ words and a link back to the service page. YouTube videos often rank on page 1 of Google inside 2–4 weeks — even when the website itself is still climbing.

Week 11 — featured snippets + FAQs

Google’s “People Also Ask” boxes and featured snippets can double CTR. We restructure existing content to capture them by adding concise, scannable Q&A sections — “How much is a gym membership in Ferndale? Answer in 50 words or less…” — plus FAQPage schema. Targets: 10 featured snippets captured by end of week.

Week 12 — referral engine + measurement

  • Launch a member referral program: existing member refers a friend → both get $25 off next month. Promoted through the email list we built in Phase 1, posted on GBP, and linked on the new /referral page.
  • Build the monthly SEO dashboard — a single Google Data Studio (free) view that shows: indexed pages, clicks, impressions, top queries, GBP views, bookings, reviews, leads, revenue attributed to organic. Auto-updates. Armory runs this forever with no agency fees.
  • Document the repeatable 30-day content cycle so Armory can keep publishing 4 posts/mo in-house after Day 90.

8. CRO — turning traffic into revenue

Ranking is half the job. Converting is the other half. This new site already does a lot of this by design, but here’s the explicit plan:

  • Every service page ends in a CTA. Not a form — a booking link. Friction down.
  • Price is shown, on every plan. “Contact us for pricing” kills conversion. You quote $43/$75/$76 — leverage that.
  • Trust signals above the fold: reviews, review count, years open, trainer certifications. The homepage stats row already does this.
  • Exit-intent lead magnets: 7-day free pass on /membership, contrast therapy trial on /recovery, 30-min NT discovery call on /nutritional-therapy.
  • A sticky mobile footer with “Call · Book” on every page.
  • Automated welcome email sequence for every lead — 3 emails, 7 days: “here’s the tour link,” “here’s what to expect on your first class,” “here’s a $25-off your first month if you join this week.”
  • Post-visit follow-up. Every tour that doesn’t convert gets a personal text from Jackie 24 hours later. Low-tech. High ROI.

11. Risks & how to avoid them

  • Google reviews policy. Never pay for reviews, never write fake ones, never cluster requests to one day. We ask one member at a time, in person, right after a win. Organic pace protects the listing from suspension.
  • Duplicate content. The current Kajabi site needs to be either redirected or deactivated after migration. If both domains live with the same content Google picks one and devalues the other. Phase 1 must include 301 redirects from the Kajabi URLs if they change.
  • Thin service-area pages. Generic “we serve X” pages get penalized. Each suburb page must carry real, unique local content — drive time, a local member quote, a local landmark reference. We build this in.
  • Slow publishing cadence. SEO rewards consistency, not bursts. After Day 90, Armory needs to publish ~2 pieces/month forever. We’ll build a 30-min content cycle Jackie can run herself (or delegate).
  • Neglected GBP after launch. The account needs one post a week and a reply to every new review within 48 hours. This is a 15-minute weekly task. Missing it is the single fastest way to lose the gains.
