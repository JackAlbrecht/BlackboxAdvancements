The Footage Problem

Most photo and video work ages in a year.Some of it ages in a week.

A brief gets typed into a deck. Ten competitors get the same deck. A crew shows up with a shot list, shoots the list, and leaves. The footage is fine. It looks like every other brand that hired the same crew with the same list.

That footage ages fast because it never had a point of view. It was a checklist, not a take. By the time the campaign is live, three competitors have already run adjacent work and everyone blurs together.

The photo and video that keeps working after a year does not come from a checklist. It comes from somebody who stayed late, waited out the light, and shot what nobody thought to ask for.

We shoot the way that does not age.
How We Shoot

We do not shoot.We wait, then we shoot.

01

Cinematic Eye

Every frame is a decision. I plan the shoot around the angle most crews would miss and work outward from there. The checklist happens; it does not lead.

02

Ruthless Patience

The right light is rarely on schedule. I arrive early, stay late, and shoot again when a cloud or a streetlamp moves. The best frame in most jobs was the sixtieth one.

03

Footage That Outlives The Product

A shoot that looks dated in a year is cheaper than a shoot that still works in five. I build both into every job so you keep hero frames long after the launch is over.

Behind The Camera
The philosophy

There is something sacred about the night. The way light bleeds across wet concrete. The way silence sits in an empty parking garage at 3:47 AM, when the city has forgotten itself for a minute. I built Long Nights Media Co around that feeling.

My first paid shoot was a friend-of-a-friend with a restored 1973 BMW and nowhere to be on a Tuesday at midnight. We drove to a loading dock in Bellingham, cut the overheads, and shot until the battery died. Six frames from that night still hang in my portfolio. One of them got me my first real client.

Ten years in, the work has not changed. I shoot for automotive builders, in-house manufacturers, brand teams, and a handful of YouTubers who want film, not social clips. If what you are making deserves to be shot by somebody who would shoot it whether you paid or not, that is my whole offer.

Fifty percent holds the date. Cancel inside seventy-two hours and the deposit stays with me. One free reschedule up to the shoot date.
