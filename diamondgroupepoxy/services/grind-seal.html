About this system

Grind and Seal Concrete Floors, done right.

Grind-and-seal is the most cost-effective way to turn a rough, dusty, or stained concrete slab into a clean, durable, easy-to-maintain floor. We run a multi-pass diamond grind to flatten the surface, open the capillaries, and expose the natural aggregate in the slab. Then we seal the whole floor with a two-coat densifier and topcoat system — either a low-sheen urethane for a matte industrial look or a higher-build polyaspartic for a semi-gloss finish. The existing concrete becomes the finished floor. No flake, no color, no resurfacing — just your slab, made permanent.

This system makes sense when the concrete is already in decent shape and the owner wants durability without the price tag of a full resinous buildup. We install it in Vancouver distribution warehouses, Sherwood machine shops, Portland loft conversions, and showroom spaces where the raw-concrete aesthetic is the point. It's also our go-to recommendation for garages where the homeowner wants dust control and stain resistance but doesn't care about color or pattern. Monthly maintenance is a dust mop and the occasional wet mop — that's it.

Where grind-and-seal shines is lifespan per dollar. Because there's no resinous wear layer to wear through, the topcoat can be refreshed every 5 to 7 years for a fraction of the original install cost, extending the life of the floor almost indefinitely. We densify the slab with a lithium silicate hardener during prep, which chemically strengthens the top quarter-inch of concrete and reduces future cracking. Combined with a high-solids topcoat, it's an incredibly low-maintenance floor.

FAQ

Grind and Seal Concrete Floors questions.

Will it hide cracks?

Hairline cracks get filled and ground flat, but grind-and-seal isn't meant to hide major damage — it celebrates the existing slab. If your concrete has serious issues we'll recommend a resinous buildup instead and tell you straight up during the quote.

Can it be done on old stained concrete?

Usually yes. Diamond grinding removes years of oil, paint, and surface staining. In cases of deep oil saturation we pre-treat with an industrial degreaser, but most slabs clean up beautifully.
